Megaflaura

Wordpress & WooCommerce

Megaflaura is an e-commerce website I made to help a local business owner in my hometown. She wanted a website that was more minimalistic than her current website.

Role

Front End Developer

Responsibilities

Redesign and build the customer facing website. Create systems that incentivize jewelers to join the platform and customers to support them on it.

Outcome

Learned how to approach a paying project. Balanced client needs with what customers need, and completed it in a timely manner and within my abilities.

Summary

Megaflaura was my first client project. This was a valuable experience that laid the foundation for my approach to developing websites for businesses.

A woman I struck up a conversation with back home told me she owned a plant store and was looking to rebuild her website. She used a Squarespace template and had few visitors to the site.

I offered to build her a website that was more of what she envisioned. I asked for store credit to pick up some plants for my dad to take care of.

We sat down and talked about what she wanted. I learned what was making customers converr and focused on that. We decided to get rid of the "Membership" page. No one signed up for it since she opened and I suggested we make her platform as lean as possible.

Sketched some ideas down and made wireframes in Figma. From there we went by the seat of our plants on colors, typefaces, and some design elements.

Used WordPress and Elementor to quickly develop and iterate.

She was happy with the result, and I was proud to have helped somoene local.

You can take a look at what we made here.

Development and Changes

We worked off of the wireframes I made and and added on to it as development went along. I found a plug in that helped organize the Store page and found some templates that were visually interesting.

We agreed to use one of the less conventional templates as long as we used a more conventional one to make it more familiar for users.

It was the same deal for typefaces. She wanted to go with an elaborate set of serif typefaces, but we comprimised on a unique sans serif font and a traditional serif font. Everything needed to be readible and accessible.

The colors mostly stayed the same. She picked out a couple colors that were not legible when they were applied to text. I made sure colored text and buttons had enough contrast to make copy and buttons ledgible.
