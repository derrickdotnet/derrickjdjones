Risouteki

Wordpress & WooCommerce

Risouteki is an e-commerce platform for independent jewelers to sell and curate small, focused collections and experimental pieces of jewelry on a premium platform.

Role

Front End Developer and UX/UI Designer

Responsibilities

Design and build the customer facing website. Create systems that incentivize jewelers to join the platform and customers to support them on it.

Outcome

Created the front end within timeframe and budget. Discovered a key insight that led to the founders making the final cut for a venture funding round.

Go to Site

Summary

Risouteki is a personal project, but I treated it like a real product.

Risouteki's goal was to make handmade fine jewelry easier to sell and buy online.

The interface's and systems' design was informed by users' needs: Having a handmade fine jewelry focused platform that is a secure and accessible way to display and sell fine jewelry. No influence from an algorythm. No need expend the time and resources needed to run an online store.

I Interviewed multiple jewelers to understand their what their biggest hurdles were. This yielded key insight into how the website can succeed.

Analyzed popular reslling websites to understand how they made their peer to peer economies.

Whipped up some sketches for the interface. Sketches became wireframes, then low and high fidelity mockups.

Used Elementor to quickly develop the site, edit it, and share it for iteration. Created custom code when needed.

You can read more about my process here.

Here's the Problem

After interviewing multiple independent fine jewelers on what their biggest pain points were when selling online, many of them had the same issues.

Large platforms like Etsy and Ebay have dropshippers who resell mass produced jewelry made for cheap, undercutting prices.

There are websites that let one sell their own handmade jewelry, their scopes are limited. Some sites only accept certain designer brands of jewelry to resell or cosign.

Platforms that sell originally made jewelry by and large are not focused on fine jewelry or aimed towards a luxury minded audience.

Building and maintaining an online store requires time and expertise that one may not have.

The website needed to have a similar level of maintenence for jewelers as an Etsy or Ebay store. Familiar and not time consuming.

Make it easy for someone to join as a jeweler or as a buyer. It needs to be part of the broader set of platforms a fine jeweler uses to sell online.

Here's My Solution

I researched sites like Depop, Vinted, Poshmark, and other platforms to understand how they attract buyers and sellers.

Their popularity is due in part to their ease of use to sign up on their peer to peer economy, commitment to making the platforms safe to use by putting security first, and backing it up with social proof.

They have similar economic structures, and all have a visual design gives a unique presence to them, they have a vibe.

How can this be applied in a fine jewelry context? I drew three things of importance to from this research: Trust, Ease, and Prestige.

Trust through making a platform is safe and attractive for people to make transactions on.

Ease in minimal friction for jewelers to showcase their work and for customers to purchase their products on the platform smoothly.

Prestige to present itself as the ideal place to show and buy handmade fine jewelry online.

Development and Changes

I went with the design I made initially. After having it reviewed by users and was given feedback.

The font for the copy was hard to read for some. Elements like the "Shop Now" button felt disctracting or overbearing.

I had testers think of this as a site like Depop for fancy jewelry, but they did not know where to sign up as a seller. This revealed where initial design failed and revealed openings for improvement.

I fell back on what I learned from the interviews: Trust, Ease, and Prestige.

I swapped out the copy's font for one that is more ledgible. Removed the "Shop Now" button. Changed the hero to have two CTAs: one for buyers and sellers, respectively. The feedback was better upon second round.

Outcome and Lessons

Risouteki is a personal project, but I treated it like a real product.

I spent days deciding what kind of problem I wanted to solve, weeks coming up with designs and rationales, weeks developing it and learning the ins and outs of Wordpress and WooCommerce.

This is a practice something from concept to complete. The case study presented represents the experience I have had with this project.

Sharing the process with regular people, folks with design experience, and coding bootcamp gave me the feedback I needed to see where my shortcomings and wrong assumptions lay. Each change slowly became a better iteration, and yielded more insight on what a usable website does for its audience.

The design and development process is a cycle. New information is only obtained through testing and feedback. Intergrating new information for each iteration is key to finding the most effective solution.
